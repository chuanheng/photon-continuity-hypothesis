# Energy-resolved X-ray transmission: proposed experiment (draft)

**Conceptual proposal only; no experiment has been performed.** Ionizing-radiation work must be conducted only in an appropriately authorized, shielded facility with qualified radiation-safety oversight.

## Objective

Determine whether a **parameterized** photon-continuity model predicts a forward energy-degraded component distinguishable from established X-ray transport and instrument backgrounds.

## Setup

- Characterized near-monochromatic incident X-rays around 60 keV.
- Aluminum targets of several measured thicknesses and a no-sample control.
- Calibrated energy-resolving detector with known angular acceptance.
- Off-axis measurements, background runs, and source spectrum characterization.
- Characterize dead time, pileup, detector escape peaks, efficiency, resolution and secondary radiation.

## Analysis

1. Measure blank and sample spectra with uncertainties.
2. Predict the full standard spectrum from validated cross sections, geometry and detector response; Beer–Lambert alone is insufficient.
3. Pre-register alternative parameters λ and α (or a loss distribution) and a detectable extra-signal region.
4. Compare energy and angle binned counts with an appropriate likelihood and systematic uncertainties.
5. Report exclusion ranges for the specified model; do not claim to prove universal photon annihilation or persistence.

## Confounders and limitations

Compton/Rayleigh scattering, fluorescence, bremsstrahlung, detector artifacts, source bandwidth and environmental background. Indistinguishable photon excitations prevent identification of an individual 'original' photon by an energy spectrum alone. An unrestricted sub-threshold survivor claim cannot be excluded by finite detector sensitivity.
